# Sharing Regent Answers

**Audience:** a GM who has an answer and wants it somewhere useful.

Every Regent answer carries three buttons. This guide covers what each one does and when it is the
right choice.

## Send to Chat

Posts the answer to the chat log as a themed card, visible to everyone.

Use it to put a rules answer in front of the table, or to share a description you want players to
read. The card takes the theme set in **Chat Card Theme**.

**The answer is posted as text, not as live Foundry content.** If the model wrote something that
looks like a document link or a dice roll, it appears as plain characters rather than a clickable
link or a roll button. That is deliberate: the model does not know your world's documents, so any
link it produces points at nothing, and a roll button nobody asked for is worse than the sentence
that described it.

## Copy

Copies the answer to your clipboard, to paste anywhere.

The plain option, and the right one when you want the text in your own notes, a journal you are
already editing, or somewhere outside Foundry.

## Create journal

Turns the answer into a journal entry.

**This works properly only for a structured answer** -- one from the Narrative or Encounter
worksheet, which asks the model for a structured reply specifically. Those become a real journal
entry with pages, images and links to the encounters described in them.

The button is offered on every answer rather than only the ones that suit it. **On an ordinary prose
answer it will not produce a useful entry**; use Copy instead and paste into a journal page you
control.

## The GM report

Separately from these three: when a **player** consults the Regent, the GM receives a whispered card
showing who asked, what they asked, and the worksheet details behind the prompt.

Players do not see it, and it is sent whether or not the player shares the answer. It exists so the
GM can see what the module is doing in their world.

## A habit worth having

**Read an answer before you share it.** Regent writes confidently whether or not it is right, and a
wrong rules answer posted to chat carries the authority of having come from the GM. The buttons are
one click; the correction is a conversation.
