# The Regent Worksheets

**Audience:** a GM who has Regent working and wants more than a chat box.

Regent has five worksheets. Each one builds a detailed prompt from your actual campaign -- your
party, your tokens, your setting -- so you get a useful answer without typing all of that context
yourself.

Switch worksheets with the tabs in the Regent window. **Regent reopens on whichever one you used
last.**

All five end the same way: fill in what you care about, submit, and the answer appears in the window
with the same buttons as any other reply.

## Lookup

**For:** rules questions, mid-session.

Choose a feature or rule, add any detail, and pick what you want back:

- **Rules** -- how it works
- **Tutorial** -- how to use it at the table

Faster than the chat box for anything mechanical, because it asks the model for a rules answer
specifically rather than a conversational one.

## Character

**For:** understanding or developing a character.

**Select a token first.** The Character worksheet fills itself from the selected token -- core
details, abilities, skills, features, weapons, spells and biography -- so it is describing a real
character rather than a hypothetical one.

Then ask for what you want: a description, a backstory, guidance on how to play them, or suggestions
for where they might go next.

## Assistant

**For:** feedback on something you have written or planned.

Set your criteria, describe the thing, and Regent responds against those criteria. Use it as a second
opinion on a plot, an encounter, a house rule, or a session plan.

## Encounter

**For:** designing a fight.

The one with the most moving parts:

- **Party** -- drag actors in, or pull in selected tokens
- **Monsters** -- drag actors in; Regent tracks total CR as you add them
- **NPCs** -- non-combatants who matter to the scene
- **Configuration** -- the difficulty you are aiming for and the shape of the fight

Regent uses party composition and monster CR together, so the answer is about *this* party rather
than a generic one of that level.

**The difficulty maths deserves a second look.** Regent is reasoning in prose, not running the
encounter-building rules, and it will state a difficulty confidently while being wrong about it.

## Narrative

**For:** writing a scene, a location, or a piece of story, and putting it into a journal.

Fill in the setting, geography, characters and rewards. Optionally include an encounter, treasure and
XP -- the defaults for those come from your settings, so you can set them once.

**Narrative answers are built to become journal entries.** The reply comes back structured, and the
**Create journal** button turns it into a real journal entry with pages, rather than a wall of text
you have to reformat. See [userguide-sharing-answers](userguide-sharing-answers.md).

## Which one to reach for

| You want | Worksheet |
|---|---|
| A rules answer, now | Lookup |
| Something about one character | Character |
| A critique of your own work | Assistant |
| A fight built for your party | Encounter |
| Prose that becomes a journal entry | Narrative |
| Anything else | The chat box |

## What Regent sends

Regent tells you. When a player submits a question, the GM receives a whispered **Regent Report**
showing who asked, what they asked, and every worksheet field that went into the prompt.

That report is for the GM's benefit -- it makes what the module is doing visible rather than
mysterious, and it is worth reading once so you know what leaves your world.
